01 · Context

Why AI readiness matters now

AI is already entering creative and cultural organisations through everyday tools, staff experimentation, suppliers, partners, audiences and creative workflows.

Advances in AI are increasing risks around cybersecurity, intellectual property, personal data and confidential information. At the same time, AI can support more efficient ways of operating, managing and communicating in the cultural sector.

Being AI ready means knowing:

01Where AI is already being used across your organisation.
02Which activities create data, IP, copyright, confidentiality or reputational risk.
03Which workflows could benefit from safe AI support.
04Which tools are approved for staff use.
05What information should never be entered into public AI tools.
06Who remains accountable for AI-assisted outputs.
07How benefits and risks will be measured.
02 · Framework

The eight areas

This diagnostic looks at eight areas of AI readiness. You score your organisation 1 to 5 in each. The result tells you whether your priority is control, structure or improvement.

01

Leadership and governance

Who owns AI risk and opportunity at executive and board level.

02

Data protection and confidentiality

What information can and cannot go into AI tools.

03

Cybersecurity, fraud and supplier risk

AI-enabled phishing, fake invoices, supplier platform exposure.

04

Approved tools and access

Which AI tools are sanctioned, for which purposes, under what conditions.

05

Staff use, skills and confidence

Whether staff use AI safely, critically and productively.

06

Workflow and productivity opportunities

Where AI could support recurring work, and how benefits are measured.

07

Knowledge, archive and reusable assets

Whether your existing policies, reports, templates and archives can be safely used as organisational knowledge.

08

Creative rights, IP and accountability

Principles for AI use involving creative works, artists' materials and public-facing content.
